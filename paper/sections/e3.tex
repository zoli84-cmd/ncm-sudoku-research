\section{E3: readability of the candidate mask}
\label{sec:e3}

\subsection{Design}
\label{sec:e3-design}

E3 asks whether the candidate mask of the puzzle can be read from the generated representation part-way through the hierarchy. The primary representation is the full-length cumulative state after level~6 of the generated codes. Level~6 is a capacity choice made before the lock: the cumulative state after level~2 is built from three codes and cannot hold a full candidate mask, whereas the level-6 state is built from many more codes; neither bound predicts readability.

The primary probes are linear, shared across cells, with nine sigmoid outputs; MLP probes appear only as secondary capacity checks. Every probe is trained on PROBE-TRAIN with binary cross-entropy and AdamW, over a fixed grid of five weight-decay values and two learning rates, with three training seeds. Each run is capped at the larger of \R{probe.epochs} epochs and the number of epochs needed for \R{probe.minsteps} optimizer steps at batch size \R{probe.batch}, with early stopping on the PROBE-VAL Brier loss; for some selected runs, among them one seed of the primary probe and every raw-input run, the best validation epoch was the last one run, so these probes might have improved with more training; a check before the lock with three times as many steps changed the level-6 contrast only marginally; the configuration with the lowest mean PROBE-VAL Brier over the seeds is selected, and the three selected seed probes are scored once on TEST. Wherever a configuration had been selected before the lock, the confirmatory run selected the same one. The primary H1 probe reads a cell together with its \R{model.peers} row, column and box peers: their states are projected on a whitened principal-component basis with \R{model.dim} components fitted on the empty cells of PROBE-TRAIN, and the cell's own state and position are appended. This cell-relative whole-layer probe replaced a per-cell probe before the lock (Section~\ref{sec:setup-stats}); the per-cell probe is kept as a secondary analysis. The metric is the Brier loss of the nine candidate bits, averaged per cell, then per puzzle over the originally empty cells, then over puzzles; each probe's per-puzzle Brier loss is the mean over its three seeds, while the thresholded and ranking metrics use the seed-averaged predictions.

The primary contrast is
\[
\theta_1 = \mathbb{E}\bigl[B_{\mathrm{permuted}} - B_{\mathrm{generated}}\bigr],
\]
where $B_{\mathrm{permuted}}$ is the Brier loss of the same probe construction retrained on representations paired with the labels of other puzzles: the representations are permuted across puzzles independently in PROBE-TRAIN and PROBE-VAL, the control selects its configuration on its own permuted validation pairs, and both probes are scored on the intact TEST pairs. A positive $\theta_1$ means that the probe trained on the intact pairing predicts the candidates of the TEST puzzles better than the control probe, which was trained and selected on pairings that carry no puzzle-specific link between representation and label. H1 has two registered readings from the same estimate: a nonzero effect when the \R{level.family}\% family interval excludes zero, and practical equivalence, readability below the threshold of \R{plan.h1margin} in Brier loss, when the \R{level.tost}\% interval lies inside $\pm$\R{plan.h1margin}. Both can hold at once. Intervals resample TEST puzzles and are conditional on the fixed training data and fitted probes; the variation across training seeds is reported separately.

\subsection{Level-6 readability (H1)}
\label{sec:e3-h1}

The level-6 probe reached a Brier loss of \R{brier.wl.gen.L06}, the permuted control \R{brier.wl.perm.L06}. The primary contrast was $\theta_1 = \R{h1.est}$ with a family interval of [\R{h1.fam.lo}, \R{h1.fam.hi}], a relative improvement of \R{h1.relimp}\% over the control; the \R{level.tost}\% interval, [\R{h1.tost.lo}, \R{h1.tost.hi}], lies inside $\pm$\R{plan.h1margin} (Tables~\ref{tab:summary} and~\ref{tab:readings}; Figure~\ref{fig:e3-readability}, right). The estimate was stable across training seeds (\R{h1.seed0}, \R{h1.seed1} and \R{h1.seed2}). Both registered readings hold: the candidate mask is readable from the level-6 state above the permuted control, but by less than the practical threshold, with the examined probe family and training budget. This does not show that the representation computes candidates, nor that it carries no candidate information; another readout might do better.

The controls place this result (Table~\ref{tab:e3-matrix}). A probe on random features of the same width reached \R{brier.wl.random}, and a probe trained on permuted labels \R{brier.wl.labelperm}; a position-wise marginal predictor fitted to the training labels, which ignores the representation, reached \R{brier.marginal}. A linear probe on surface features of the puzzle alone (clue count, clue digit frequencies and cell position) reached \R{brier.surface}, a lower Brier loss than the level-6 generated state (it also had a higher PR-AUC, but no thresholded F1 above zero; Table~\ref{tab:secondary}). The same construction on the teacher's level-6 state, computed from the solution rather than the puzzle, gave a similar observed Brier loss (\R{brier.teacher.L06}).

The candidate mask is almost exactly readable from the puzzle itself: a linear probe on the raw input, the one-hot contents of the cell and of its \R{model.peers} peers, reached \R{brier.raw.proj} on whitened principal-component scores of these features fitted on PROBE-TRAIN (projected) and \R{brier.raw.unproj} on the features themselves (unprojected), a small MLP on the raw input \R{brier.raw.mlp}, a linear probe on peer digit counts \R{brier.peercounts}, and the exact rule is exact by construction. The registered raw-input contrasts, raw-input probe minus generated level-6 state, were \R{rawgen.proj.est} (projected; \R{level.ci95}\% interval [\R{rawgen.proj.ci.lo}, \R{rawgen.proj.ci.hi}]) and \R{rawgen.unproj.est} (unprojected; [\R{rawgen.unproj.ci.lo}, \R{rawgen.unproj.ci.hi}]) in Brier loss. The label is therefore directly accessible from the input, and the level-6 state carries much less of it in linearly readable form than the input does, and in Brier loss less than even its surface statistics; this does not mean that the latent space contains no logical information.

The registered secondary metrics also place the primary probe far below the raw-input probes, with a macro F1 of \R{sec.wl.f1} against \R{sec.raw.f1} for the projected raw-input probe, and the per-cell probe gave a similarly small effect (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-e3}). The training-size curve of the primary probe was still falling at the largest size, from \R{ts.wl.n125} with \R{ts.size.min} training puzzles to \R{ts.wl.n2000} with \R{ts.size.max} (Figure~\ref{fig:e3-supporting} in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-e3}, left), so a larger training set might read somewhat more.

\subsection{Levels 2 and 12}
\label{sec:e3-levels}

At level~2 the whole-layer probe gave $\theta_1 = \R{theta1.wl.L02.est}$ (\R{level.ci95}\% interval [\R{theta1.wl.L02.ci.lo}, \R{theta1.wl.L02.ci.hi}]; Table~\ref{tab:levels}). At level~12 the candidate mask was clearly readable ($\theta_1 = \R{theta1.wl.L12.est}$, \R{level.ci95}\% interval [\R{theta1.wl.L12.ci.lo}, \R{theta1.wl.L12.ci.hi}]). Still, the teacher's level-12 state, which encodes the solution, was read equally well: the difference teacher minus generated was \R{thetaT.est}, with a \R{level.thetaTtost}\% interval of [\R{thetaT.tost.lo}, \R{thetaT.tost.hi}] inside $\pm$\R{plan.thetaTmargin}. The level-12 readability is therefore consistent with solution content; it does not show that the generated representation computes candidates or encodes inference steps.

A secondary exploratory unit-level target, whether a digit is already placed in a given row, column or box, was at chance from the generated level-6 state (ROC-AUC \R{unit.gen.auc}) and perfectly ranked from the raw input (ROC-AUC \R{unit.raw.auc}); the construction and the full result are in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-e3}.

\subsection{Equivariance of the level-12 readout (exploratory)}
\label{sec:e3-equiv}

In this exploratory analysis without a registered criterion, the level-12 readout was not equivariant under sampled Sudoku symmetries: the mean deviation between the readout of a transformed puzzle and the transformed readout of the original was \R{equiv.dev.all} under the full symmetry group, against a tolerance of \R{plan.equivtol} fixed before the lock, while the Brier losses on transformed and original puzzles were similar (\R{equiv.brier.trans} and \R{equiv.brier.orig}); the registration stated this pattern, seen on PROBE-VAL, as the expected outcome. The design and the full result are in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-e3} and Table~\ref{tab:equivariance}. It does not show that the model lacks symmetry-consistent representations in general.
