\section{E3: readability of the candidate mask}
\label{sec:e3}

\subsection{Design}
\label{sec:e3-design}

E3 asks whether the candidate mask of the puzzle can be read from the generated representation part-way through the hierarchy. The primary representation is the full-length cumulative state after level~6 of the generated codes. Level~6 is a capacity choice made before the lock: the cumulative state after level~2 is built from three codes and cannot hold a full candidate mask, whereas the level-6 state is built from many more codes; neither bound predicts readability.

All probes are linear, shared across cells, with nine sigmoid outputs, trained on PROBE-TRAIN, selected on PROBE-VAL and scored once on TEST, with three training seeds and a fixed grid of regularization settings. The primary H1 probe reads a cell together with its \R{model.peers} row, column and box peers: their states are projected on a whitened principal-component basis fitted on PROBE-TRAIN, and the cell's own state and position are appended. This cell-relative whole-layer probe replaced a per-cell probe before the lock, because levels below 12 reach the cell positions only by interpolation; the per-cell probe is kept as a secondary analysis. The metric is the Brier loss of the nine candidate bits, averaged per cell, then per puzzle over the originally empty cells, then over puzzles.

The primary contrast is
\[
\theta_1 = \mathbb{E}\bigl[B_{\mathrm{permuted}} - B_{\mathrm{generated}}\bigr],
\]
where $B_{\mathrm{permuted}}$ is the Brier loss of the same probe construction retrained on representations paired with the labels of other puzzles. A positive $\theta_1$ means the generated state predicts the puzzle's own candidates better than an unrelated state does. H1 has two registered readings from the same estimate: a nonzero effect when the \R{level.family}\% family interval excludes zero, and practical equivalence, readability below the threshold of \R{plan.h1margin} in Brier loss, when the \R{level.tost}\% interval lies inside $\pm$\R{plan.h1margin}. Both can hold at once.

\subsection{Level-6 readability (H1)}
\label{sec:e3-h1}

The level-6 probe reached a Brier loss of \R{brier.wl.gen.L06}, the permuted control \R{brier.wl.perm.L06}. The primary contrast was $\theta_1 = \R{h1.est}$ with a family interval of [\R{h1.fam.lo}, \R{h1.fam.hi}], a relative improvement of \R{h1.relimp}\% over the control; the \R{level.tost}\% interval, [\R{h1.tost.lo}, \R{h1.tost.hi}], lies inside $\pm$\R{plan.h1margin} (Table~\ref{tab:primary}; Figure~\ref{fig:e3-readability}, right). Both registered readings hold: the candidate mask is readable from the level-6 state above the permuted control, but by less than the practical threshold, with the examined probe family and training budget. This does not show that the representation computes candidates, nor that it carries no candidate information; another readout might do better.

The controls bound this result from both sides (Table~\ref{tab:e3-matrix}). A probe on random features of the same width reached \R{brier.wl.random}, and a probe trained on permuted labels \R{brier.wl.labelperm}; a label-free predictor that outputs the marginal candidate rate reached \R{brier.marginal}. The same construction on the teacher's level-6 state, computed from the solution rather than the puzzle, gave \R{brier.teacher.L06}, practically equal to the generated state. In contrast, the candidate mask is almost exactly readable from the puzzle itself: a linear probe on the raw input reached \R{brier.raw.proj} (projected) and \R{brier.raw.unproj} (unprojected), a small MLP on the raw input and the exact rule both reached \R{brier.rule}, and linear probes on peer digit counts \R{brier.peercounts}. The label is therefore directly accessible from the input, and the level-6 state carries far less of it in linearly readable form than the input does; this does not mean that the latent space contains no logical information.

The per-cell probe gave a similarly small effect ($\theta_1 = \R{theta1.pc.L06.est}$, \R{level.ci95}\% interval [\R{theta1.pc.L06.ci.lo}, \R{theta1.pc.L06.ci.hi}]), and a per-cell MLP did not improve on the linear probe (\R{brier.pc.mlp.L06} against \R{brier.pc.lin.L06}). The training-size curve of the primary probe was still falling at the largest size, from \R{ts.wl.n125} with \R{ts.size.min} training puzzles to \R{ts.wl.n2000} with \R{ts.size.max}
(Figure~\ref{fig:e3-supporting}, left), so a larger training set might read somewhat more. By inference depth (Figure~\ref{fig:e3-supporting}, right), the Brier loss rose from \R{depth.wl.d1} for cells filled in the first solver wave to \R{depth.wl.d4} for cells filled in wave 4 or later; the positive label rate rose with depth as well, from \R{depth.pos.d1}\% to \R{depth.pos.d4}\%, so the depth breakdown is descriptive and does not indicate a deeper algorithm.

\subsection{Levels 2 and 12}
\label{sec:e3-levels}

At level~2 the whole-layer probe gave $\theta_1 = \R{theta1.wl.L02.est}$. At level~12 the candidate mask was clearly readable ($\theta_1 = \R{theta1.wl.L12.est}$, \R{level.ci95}\% interval [\R{theta1.wl.L12.ci.lo}, \R{theta1.wl.L12.ci.hi}]), but the teacher's level-12 state, which encodes the solution, was read equally well: the difference teacher minus generated was \R{thetaT.est}, with a \R{level.thetaTtost}\% interval of [\R{thetaT.tost.lo}, \R{thetaT.tost.hi}] inside $\pm$\R{plan.thetaTmargin}. The level-12 readability is therefore consistent with solution content; it does not show that the generated representation computes candidates or encodes inference steps. A unit-level target (whether a digit is already placed in a row, column or box) was not readable from the generated level-6 state (ROC-AUC \R{unit.gen.auc}) but perfectly from the raw input (\R{unit.raw.auc}).

\subsection{Equivariance of the level-12 readout (exploratory)}
\label{sec:e3-equiv}

We applied Sudoku symmetries (digit permutations and grid transformations) to \R{equiv.n} TEST puzzles, ran the model on the transformed puzzles and compared the level-12 readout with the transformed readout of the original. Accuracy was unchanged (Brier \R{equiv.brier.trans} on transformed against \R{equiv.brier.orig} on original puzzles), but the per-cell predictions moved: the mean deviation was \R{equiv.dev.all}, against a tolerance of \R{plan.equivtol} fixed from seed noise before the lock, and none of the transformed puzzles stayed within it (Table~\ref{tab:equivariance}). This analysis is exploratory and has no registered criterion. It shows that per-cell predictions change under symmetry while accuracy does not; it does not show that the model lacks symmetry-consistent representations in general.
