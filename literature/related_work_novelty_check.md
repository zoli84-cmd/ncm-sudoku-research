# Related-work novelty check for E2, E3, E4

**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Scope:** prior work relevant to the planned contributions in `research_plan_v3.1.md` (§1, §2.1, §3, §5, §7–§9).
**Tools:** Consensus API (`/v1/search`, 17 calls), Exa search and contents API (`EXA_API_KEY` was set, so Exa was used instead of WebSearch/WebFetch), arXiv export API (keyword and id-list queries), arXiv HTML full text for four papers, Semantic Scholar citation endpoint (2 calls). The full query list is in [query_log.md](query_log.md).
**Status:** this is a search record, not a plan decision. Everything under "Implications for the plan" is a suggestion. Any adoption must be logged in `DECISIONS.md`.

## Summary

- **E2 (level sensitivity, four-branch compensation): partially covered.** Perturbing early codes in coarse-to-fine generators is not new. The NCM paper itself discusses early-code interventions and trains with corruption of earlier levels (plan §2.1, [1] App. F.3, H). Work on Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models treats cross-scale error propagation as a known failure mode (Bi et al. 2026; Chen et al. 2026), but it builds correction methods and does not measure the effect. The closest NCM-specific work is Khoury (2026, Zenodo, not peer reviewed). He shows that coarse NCM codes are strongly non-canonical and that regenerating the fine levels from re-encoded coarse levels changes output quality. I found no work that runs a fixed-suffix vs. regenerated-suffix contrast (branches A–D) at equal discrete edit cost, or any level-wise sensitivity study on Sudoku.
- **E3 (candidate-mask and depth probes, equivariance): partially covered, and the overlap is substantial for the label.** Kniazev & Fijalkow (2026) linearly probe Sudoku candidates in a trace-trained transformer. Their per-cell candidate probes plateau at about 80%, while unit-level (row/column/box × digit) probes reach perfect exact match. Shah et al. (NeurIPS 2024) report candidate-set "probing", but they actually force the output and compare its top-k against the solver, not a probe trained on embeddings. Rodrigues & Kang (2026) probe HRM on Sudoku (cell digit, violation flags) and find that the probe directions are not causally special. Still new as far as I found: probing a discrete residual-VQ cumulative state level by level, synchronous naked/hidden-single wave-depth labels, and equivariance tests under the Sudoku symmetry group with re-run inputs.
- **E4 (targeted patching for the forced digit, minimal counterfactual pairs): partially covered.** Kniazev & Fijalkow (2026) find a "naked-single circuit" of final-MLP neurons with causal patching, which is the closest prior work to E4a's target variable, though in an autoregressive trace model. Rodrigues & Kang (2026) do state interventions and cross-puzzle patching in HRM on Sudoku. I found no study that uses Sudoku minimal counterfactual clue-edit pairs (E4b), and none that patches a coarse-to-fine discrete-code generator.
- **Overall framing (interpretability of NCM on Sudoku): novel as far as I found, with caveats.** The arXiv full-text query `"next-concept modeling"` returns only the NCM paper. Semantic Scholar lists 0 citations. The only follow-ups I found are three unreviewed Zenodo preprints and blog posts (Khoury; Vimal; Music). None of them does mechanistic or probing work on Sudoku. Vimal (2026) does a descriptive level-by-level analysis of NCM on XSum.
- **The closest papers overall:** (1) Kniazev & Fijalkow 2026; (2) Khoury 2026; (3) Rodrigues & Kang 2026; (4) Shah et al. 2024; (5) Komissarov 2026.
- **Methodology risk shared by recent work:** several 2025–2026 papers show that decodable variables are not causally used, including in Sudoku/HRM. This supports the plan's choice to keep decodability, generalization, and causal role as separate levels of evidence (§1).
- **Terminology risk for "compensation":** the self-repair literature (McGrath et al. 2023; Rushing & Nanda 2024; Ahmad et al. 2026) studies a closely related phenomenon inside one forward pass. Ahmad et al. argue that apparent repair can be a pre-existing "counterweight" gain, which supports the plan's choice not to equate compensation with error correction (§1).

## Implications for the plan (suggestions, not decisions)

1. **Cite Kniazev & Fijalkow (2026) as the closest prior work for E3 and E4a.** Its naked-single neurons promote the forced digit, which is close to the E4 target variable. Present E3/E4 as testing whether a similar logical readout exists in a *non-autoregressive, discrete, level-indexed* latent. Do not claim novelty for "Sudoku candidates are linearly decodable".
2. **Consider a unit-level label as a secondary E3 target or control.** Their per-cell candidate probes plateau (about 80%) while row/column/box × digit presence probes are exact. A secondary probe for presence in the 27 units × 9 digits on $Z_{\leq\ell}$ would show whether a weak per-cell result reflects the representation's organization rather than missing information. It would need to be pre-specified before TEST (plan §8.3 secondary metrics).
3. **Cite Shah et al. (2024) accurately.** Their "candidate set" evidence comes from forcing outputs and comparing top-k with the solver, not from a learned probe on embeddings. If cited as "linear probing", the description should be corrected.
4. **Use HRM (Rodrigues & Kang 2026) as the precedent for "decodable but not causally special" on Sudoku.** Consider adding a size-matched random-direction ablation control to any probe-direction intervention in E4, in addition to the equal-size control region already in §9.2.
5. **Cite Khoury (2026) in E1/E2, with the caveat that it is not peer reviewed.** It reports that NCM's generated coarse codes are often non-canonical (61–96% of codes rewritten on re-encoding at the coarse levels, same decoded text). This supports the plan's statement that teacher/generated code mismatch is not a logical error (§6). It also suggests that code-level Hamming may be a weak proxy for semantic damage. An optional diagnostic: re-encode the decoded outputs of branches A–D and report code-level vs. decoded distance.
6. **Frame H3 explicitly against the training procedure.** NCM is trained with corruption of earlier levels (plan §2.1 [1] App. H; Music's TinyStories ablation shows this shifts robustness). A positive $\theta_{\mathrm{comp}}$ can therefore be read as measuring a property the training objective was designed to produce, not an emergent one.
7. **Relate "compensation" to the self-repair literature.** Cite Hydra / Rushing & Nanda / Ahmad et al. The latter's "every ablation is a dose" view parallels the dose curves in §7.3, and its counterweight explanation is another reason to keep "compensation" separate from "error correction".
8. **E4 patching methodology.** Add Heimersheim & Nanda (2024) and Vaidyanathan et al. (2026) next to Zhang & Nanda. Because interaction effects grow with the clean–patched distance, report that distance (already partly covered by "norms" in §9.2) and treat large-distance patches cautiously.
9. **E3 equivariance (H4).** Consider citing Komissarov (2026), which shows that digit permutation strongly changes the behavior of a Sudoku neural solver. Optionally, add a representation-level consistency measure (CKA/NMI, Schlesinger et al. 2026) as an exploratory metric next to the probe-output equivariance in §8.4.
10. **Novelty statement for the overall framing.** "First interpretability study of NCM on Sudoku" is defensible on present evidence, but should be hedged ("to our knowledge, as of 2026-11-08"). The search should be repeated before submission, because NCM appeared on 2026-10-09 and follow-ups are arriving quickly (three Zenodo preprints within three weeks).

## Limitations of this search

- Consensus has little coverage of 2026 ML preprints, and Zenodo/blog items are not indexed there. Most relevant hits came from Exa and arXiv. Consensus was used for 17 of the 25 allowed calls.
- Exa is semantic search with ranked top-12 results. Relevant papers using different vocabulary (e.g., "Latin square", "constraint propagation" without "Sudoku") may be missed.

- Full text was read only for four arXiv papers (2409.10502, 2605.18847, 2609.22197, 2607.19635), and even those were keyword-searched, not read in full. All other entries are verified at the abstract level only. Quantitative claims in the table come from abstracts or the quoted passages.
- The NCM paper's own appendix content (F.3, H) was not re-checked here; the summary relies on plan §2.1.
- The three NCM follow-ups (Khoury, Vimal, Music) are unreviewed Zenodo preprints, and some of their authors report AI-assisted drafting. Treat their numbers as unverified.
- Workshop papers, non-English venues, and talks were not searched. Papers posted after 2026-11-08 are obviously not covered.
